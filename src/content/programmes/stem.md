## Science that does not come out of a textbook

By five, a child has moved past "what is this?" and into "what happens if?" That is the exact question science is made of, and it is usually the moment formal schooling starts replacing it with copying off a board.

STEM Club exists to keep that question alive. Children aged 5 to 8 spend two hours every Saturday building, breaking, measuring and arguing about why something did not work the first time.

## What we actually do

### Science
Real investigations with a genuine unknown at the end. Density towers, red cabbage pH indicator, growing crystals, dissecting a flower under a loupe. Children write a prediction before they start — and we make a point of celebrating the wrong ones, because a disproved prediction is the most useful thing that can happen in a lab.

### Technology
Screens come last, not first. Children start with **unplugged coding**: sequencing cards, giving each other precise movement instructions, discovering that "walk forward" needs a number attached. Once they own the logic, we move to block-based coding.

### Engineering
Spaghetti towers, paper bridges tested to destruction with weights, marble runs, balloon-powered cars, simple hydraulics from syringes and tubing. The brief is always the same shape: here is a constraint, here are the materials, make it work.

### Mathematics
Not sums. Measurement with real instruments, symmetry and tessellation, estimation games, reading their own experiment data onto a graph. Mathematics as the tool you need to answer your own question.

## Why teams of three

Every build is done in threes. It is the smallest group where a child cannot quietly do nothing and cannot quietly do everything. They have to explain their idea, hear a competing one, and settle it — which is most of what engineering actually is.

## A typical Saturday

| Time | What is happening |
| --- | --- |
| 9:00 | The Challenge — today's question or brief, plus predictions on the board |
| 9:20 | Build and test in teams of three |
| 10:10 | Break, fruit, and running around outside |
| 10:25 | Iterate — you have seen it fail once, now make it better |
| 10:50 | Show and tell: each team demonstrates and says what they would change |

## Who it is for

Children aged 5 to 8, whether or not they attended Tiny Steps as a preschooler. Many of our STEM Club families come from other primary schools nearby and simply want their child doing something on a Saturday morning that is not a tuition class.

No prior experience is needed, and children who describe themselves as "not good at science" are exactly who this is designed for.

> Nobody is graded, nothing is memorised, and every child leaves holding a thing they made.
