## The first time you leave them is harder for you than for them

Most families who come to us for Playgroup are doing this for the very first time. The question underneath every enquiry is not really about curriculum. It is: *will my child be alright without me?*

So we built our Playgroup around that question rather than around a syllabus.

## How settling-in actually works

We do not ask you to hand your child over at the gate on day one. Our settling-in runs over roughly two weeks, and it moves at your child's pace, not ours.

- **Days 1–3** — you stay in the room the whole session. Your child explores; you are the safe base they return to.
- **Days 4–6** — you move to the reading corner just outside. Still visible, still reachable.
- **Week 2** — you leave for 30 minutes, then an hour, then the full session.

Some children skip ahead. Some need a third week. Both are completely normal, and neither costs you anything extra.

## What a morning looks like

A one-year-old does not learn from being talked at. They learn through their hands, their mouth, their whole body. So the room is set up as a series of invitations rather than a timetable.

| Time | What is happening |
| --- | --- |
| 8:30 | Arrival, shoes off, hello song |
| 8:45 | Free exploration — sensory trays, stacking, posting, pouring |
| 9:30 | Snack, served family-style so children pour and pass |
| 10:00 | Garden time — climbing, sand, water, bare feet |
| 10:45 | Story, rhymes and songs in all three languages |
| 11:15 | Tidy up together, then home |

## Why the ratio matters more than the equipment

Every school will show you photographs of nice wooden toys. Far fewer will tell you how many adults are in the room.

We keep Playgroup at **one adult to four children**. At this age that number is the single biggest determinant of whether a child gets responded to when they need something — a nappy, a cuddle, a word for the thing they are pointing at. Beautiful materials do not comfort a crying toddler. People do.

## What to bring

Two changes of clothes, a labelled water bottle, nappies if you use them, and one comfort item from home — a cloth, a soft toy, anything that smells like you. We keep it in their basket and they can reach for it whenever they need to.

> Your child does not need to be toilet trained, speak in sentences, or be "ready". Readiness is what we build together.
