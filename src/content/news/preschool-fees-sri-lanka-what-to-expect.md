Almost no preschool in Colombo publishes its fees. You ring, you are invited in, and the number arrives at the end of a warm hour-long visit — which is, of course, the point.

We think that is the wrong way round, so this article explains how preschool pricing is actually structured here, and gives you the questions that produce a comparable number.

## Why the advertised number is never the real number

Schools quote a monthly or termly tuition figure. The annual cost is usually made up of considerably more than that:

**Admission / registration fee.** A one-off payment on joining. Often substantial, frequently non-refundable, and almost never included in the quoted rate.

**Term fees vs monthly fees.** Some schools bill three terms a year, some bill twelve months. Three terms sounds cheaper and often is not — check whether the "holiday" months are still charged.

**Materials and resources.** A per-term or annual charge for consumables.

**Meals and snacks.** Included at some schools, extra at others, and sometimes charged per day.

**Uniform.** Where required, usually from a specified supplier.

**Events and outings.** Concerts, sports day, field trips, graduation. Individually small, collectively not.

**Extended hours / daycare.** If you need cover past midday, this is a separate line and often the largest one.

**The annual increase.** Fees typically rise each year. If you are planning three years, this compounds.

## The one question that cuts through all of it

> "What is the total amount I will pay you between now and this time next year, including everything?"

Ask for it in writing. A school that will not put an annual total in an email is telling you something.

Then ask the follow-up:

> "And what was last year's increase, in percent?"

## Comparing two schools properly

Build the same four lines for each. Anything else is guesswork.

| | School A | School B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-off admission fee | | |
| Tuition, full year | | |
| Extras (meals, materials, uniform, events) | | |
| Extended hours, if needed | | |
| **Total year one** | | |
| **Total year two** (no admission fee, plus increase) | | |

Year two is worth calculating separately. Schools with a high admission fee and low tuition look expensive in year one and cheap thereafter — which may suit you well if you are staying three years.

## What actually drives the price

Understanding this helps you judge whether a higher fee is buying anything real.

**Ratio is the big one.** Staff are the dominant cost in any preschool. A school running 1:4 for toddlers has roughly twice the staffing cost per child of one running 1:8. If two schools are the same price and one has half the ratio, the difference is going somewhere else.

**Teacher qualifications and retention.** Trained, experienced, well-paid teachers who stay are expensive and are the thing most worth paying for.

**Space and outdoor area.** Land in Dehiwala is not cheap. A real garden is a genuine cost.

**Materials.** A complete set of Montessori materials is a serious capital outlay. It is also a one-off, so it should not justify a permanently higher fee on its own.

Things that drive price *without* driving quality: marketing, elaborate buildings, a long list of outsourced extracurriculars you did not ask for.

## Reasonable questions about money

None of these are rude. Ask them plainly.

- Is any part of the admission fee refundable if we leave in the first term?
- What notice must I give before withdrawing, and do I pay for that period?
- Is there a sibling discount?
- Can I pay monthly rather than per term?
- What happens to fees if my child is off sick for three weeks?
- What happens if the school closes for an extended period?
- Is there any scholarship or assisted place available?

That last one is worth asking even if you assume the answer is no. Some schools hold quiet places and never advertise them.

## What is not worth paying extra for

At preschool age, be sceptical of premiums attached to:

- **Tablets and smartboards.** Under-fives learn through their hands. Screens in a preschool are a marketing feature.
- **Long extracurricular lists.** Often outsourced, often 30 minutes once a week, often not very good.
- **Early formal academics.** Being drilled on worksheets at three buys a short-lived head start that evens out by around age eight, and can cost the child their appetite for the work.
- **Very large premises.** Children experience one room and one garden.
